Dallas County, Texas

Dallas addiction aftercare and sober companion planning

A residential program may schedule the next appointment while the person returning home still has to work out the rest of the week. Use this guide to map the gaps around treatment, find Dallas County recovery resources and compare what extra support would actually cover.

Where to start

Ask the residential team to confirm the outpatient intake and explain the recommended schedule. Then ask the person returning home where practical help would make a difference. Dallas County residents can call the regional Outreach, Screening, Assessment and Referral line at 844-275-0600 to ask about local substance use services. [1]

Map the move from residential care to home

Start with the treatment team's recommendations and build the weekly schedule in reverse. Add the appointments, the time it takes to reach them from home or work, and who will help if a ride or provider falls through.

  • Get a receiving contactAsk the residential program who will send the records, who at the next program will confirm receipt and when the intake happens. For a move back to Dallas from out of state, confirm which Texas provider will handle follow-up and what the sending program can do while the referral is pending.
  • Plan the hours outside careTalk through the first evening alone, the first shift back at work, and the first weekend. For each, write one activity the person has chosen, one person they can contact, and one backup if the plan falls through.
  • Check the commute before committingAn afternoon intake in Dallas and evening obligations in another city may leave little room for a missed ride or an extended visit. Ask whether the program's appointment times or format fit the route from home, work and other standing obligations.

Choose support for the gap you can name

Compare who will do the work, during which hours and with whose permission. Agree in advance when to review whether the support is still useful.

  • Outpatient treatmentAsk the treating team about the recommended level of care. Have the receiving program explain its schedule, care team, payment terms and what it needs from the prior program.
  • Peer recovery supportAsk a local program about trained peers with lived experience, community meetings or help finding transportation and sober housing. Texas Health and Human Services publishes a resource map for recovery support. [2]
  • Private sober companionCompare in-person support for specific routines such as getting to appointments or planning high-risk evenings. Ask for agreed hours, awake versus on-call overnight coverage, backup staffing, privacy boundaries, rates and how the companion will communicate with the treating team. Keep medication and treatment decisions with licensed care providers.

Use the Dallas County referral and prevention resources

Give the referral line the actual county of residence. Texas lists separate regional contacts, so someone staying on the Tarrant County side of the metroplex can use the state directory for that county. [1]

  • Find a substance use serviceCall 844-275-0600 for the North Texas Behavioral Health Authority's Dallas-area referral service. Have a ZIP code and the recommended next level of care handy. Ask about eligibility, intake, cost and what to do if no opening is available. [1]
  • Ask about overdose preventionDallas County Health and Human Services publishes local naloxone and training resources and offers follow-up support for people at risk of overdose. For a suspected overdose, call 911 immediately. [3] [4]
  • Keep crisis contacts at handCall or text 988 for emotional, suicidal or substance use-related crisis support. Call 911 for immediate danger or a medical emergency. [4]

Common questions

Where can I get an addiction treatment referral in Dallas County?

Texas Health and Human Services lists 844-275-0600 as the regional Outreach, Screening, Assessment and Referral number for Dallas County and several nearby counties. Ask about screening, referrals and the options available for your ZIP code. [1]

What should I ask before hiring a sober companion in Dallas?

Ask who will be assigned, what hours they cover, how relief is staffed, what information they share with family or clinicians and the total cost including travel and overnight charges. Ask how the engagement ends or changes if the person wants a different companion.

Can a sober companion support me while I attend an outpatient program?

You can ask for practical support around the outpatient schedule, such as an agreed ride or help keeping a routine. Have the outpatient team handle clinical decisions and agree on what the companion may share with the team.

Is community recovery support available after residential treatment?

Texas Health and Human Services describes peer recovery support before, during and after treatment. Use the state's resource map to look for programs and ask each program about availability and eligibility. [2]
